Hispanic community & connection

“Se habla español” should lead to a real conversation

By Carmen Garcia Tirado · November 5, 2026 · 2 min read

Leer el resumen en español ↓

A Spanish invitation opens a door. What happens when someone walks through it?

That is the question I want local businesses to ask before putting “Se habla español” on the next flyer. It is a useful promise when the business has a clear way to continue the conversation.

Make the welcome practical

My take: start with the customer experience you can reliably provide. If Spanish-speaking help is available at particular times, say so. If a team member returns those calls, explain how the person can request that callback.

A small, dependable process gives a customer something they can use. An unanswered message leaves the invitation unfinished.

Ask instead of assuming

A surname, appearance, or accent does not tell you how someone wants to communicate. Ask: “Would you prefer English or Spanish?” Give the person room to choose.

Some customers may prefer to talk in Spanish and read details in English, or the other way around. Let the individual guide you. Hispanic communities include different backgrounds and experiences; one approach will not fit everyone.

Follow the whole path

Read your invitation, inquiry form, confirmation, and reply together. Can a person understand the service, ask a question, and receive a clear next step?

  • Use direct, familiar wording.
  • Have a fluent reviewer check the public-facing translation.
  • Explain who responds and when language assistance is available.
  • Use appropriately qualified help for specialized documents or advice.

A resource worth asking about

The Florida SBDC at UNF states that language assistance services are available for people with limited English proficiency. It also serves Marion County. See the SBDC’s published service information. Ask the provider how assistance can be arranged for your needs.

That is one example of the connection I want Cafecito to make: helping people find an available resource and understand the next step.

One thing to improve this week

Review the first response a Spanish-speaking customer receives from your business. Fix one point where the conversation could stop.

I want our businesses to be easier to approach and our community to have clearer paths to the help it needs. Listening and following through are good places to begin.

Resumen en español

“Se habla español” debe llevar a una conversación real. Pregunta el idioma preferido, explica cuándo hay atención disponible y revisa el proceso completo, desde la invitación hasta la respuesta. El Florida SBDC en UNF publica que ofrece asistencia lingüística para personas con dominio limitado del inglés; consulta cómo solicitarla.
