Bilingual connections

Connecting in two languages begins with listening

By The Liaison Companies · Cafecito with Carmen · November 5, 2026

Leer el resumen en español ↓

A translated flyer can help someone understand an offer. A welcoming conversation helps them decide whether to take the next step. Businesses trying to connect across languages need to think about both.

Ask about language preferences

Give people an easy way to tell you which language they prefer. Do not guess based on a name, accent, or appearance. Some people prefer one language for conversation and another for written details. Let their preference guide the interaction.

Look at the whole experience

Read your invitation, registration form, confirmation, and follow-up together. If you invite someone to ask questions in Spanish, make sure there is a realistic way to answer them. Be clear about when bilingual help is available and who will respond.

Make the next step understandable

Use plain language. Explain what you offer, what happens after an inquiry, and how to contact your business. Have a fluent reviewer check public-facing translations, especially details that affect a customer’s decision. Technical or regulated information may need a qualified specialist.

Build relationships before asking for attention

Learn from the people you hope to reach. Ask which questions keep coming up and what makes it difficult to participate. Show up consistently, follow through on commitments, and treat feedback as information you can use.

Start by reviewing one customer journey in both languages. Can someone understand the offer, ask a question, and receive a response? Fix the point where the conversation stops.

Resumen en español

Conectar en dos idiomas empieza por escuchar

Una traducción ayuda, pero la experiencia completa también importa. Pregunta en qué idioma la persona prefiere comunicarse. No lo supongas por su nombre, apariencia o acento.

Revisa la invitación, el formulario, la confirmación y el seguimiento. Si ofreces atención en español, explica cómo y cuándo estará disponible. Usa lenguaje claro y pide a una persona con dominio del idioma que revise los textos públicos.

La confianza se construye escuchando y cumpliendo. Empieza por revisar si una persona puede entender tu oferta, hacer una pregunta y recibir una respuesta en el idioma que prefiere.
